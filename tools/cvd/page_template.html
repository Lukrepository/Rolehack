Colour Vision in Rolehack

What a colour-blind player loses in Rolehack today, what other games and the guidelines do about it, and seven decisions to make before anything is built. Every swatch below is computed from the colours in the code.

What happened next (2026-09-28): Lucas took all seven leans, and all three layers were built the same day on the phone and the web and confirmed by him on the phone: safe red-green defaults (layer 1), the Colour vision setting (layer 2), and vanilla's glyph: colours and symbols (layer 3). Then Q6 changed: Lucas asked for the tiles too, and each mode now has its own tile palette as well (the companion page, Colour Vision Tiles). A colour-blind playtester (Q7) is still to be found, so on 2026-10-08 Lucas marked the setting beta on both builds: it is tuned by simulation, and no colour-blind player has checked it yet. On the web it sits in Settings' own Accessibility box. The swatches below show Rolehack as it was before any of this. One count was corrected after publishing: the web palette's same-letter collisions are 155, not 134.

An example: the same status lines and inventory, drawn with Rolehack's colours (the inventory in the web build's palette). For a deuteranope, Hungry and Burdened wear one colour, and the blessed knife and the cursed potion look alike. Every word is still there; what goes is the glance.

The short answer

  • Rolehack's own controls mostly pass already. Every key is labelled and never moves, the lamps are labelled and switch by brightness, a layer names itself in its chip, a badge spells out its condition, and search mode and the armed state light a labelled lamp instead of recolouring keys.
  • Five signals still lean on colour: the HP colour, the hero's outline on the phone map, the badge tiers, the blessed/uncursed/cursed menu colours, and the map itself. Two collide for deuteranopes: the badge tiers (serious against warning) and the web build's blessed against cursed. The badge tiers also collide for deuteranomaly, the commonest type.
  • The map is the big one. In ASCII, same-letter monsters differ only by colour: 155 pairs collapse for red-green viewers in the web palette, 37 in the phone's. In tiles, %%NLOST%% pairs are the same sprite recoloured and merge, cockatrice and pyrolisk among them.
  • The skins don't own the risky colours. HP, badges, menu colours, the map palette and the tiles are shared by Terminal, Terminal light and GameCube alike, so making one skin safe would miss most of the problem.
  • A per-type filter would repeat the phone. Android, iOS and Windows already offer protan, deutan, tritan and greyscale filters, the first two with an intensity slider. What only the game can do is choose its own colours and add second cues.
  • My lean: safe defaults for red-green in every skin, a Colour vision setting that swaps palettes (with the standard names), and letting vanilla's own glyph: option work on both builds. Tiles can wait for a colour-blind playtester.
1 · Who this is for

The types of colour blindness

Colour vision rests on three kinds of cone. An anomalous trichromat has all three but one is shifted, and sees the usual confusions more weakly. A dichromat is missing one kind outright. The protan types involve the long-wavelength (red) cones, so reds also look darker; deutan types the middle (green) cones; tritan types the short (blue) cones, which confuses blue with green and yellow with violet. Achromatopsia (rod monochromacy) sees no colour at all, and usually comes with light sensitivity and low acuity.

Figures are Kalloniatis and Luu's summary for populations of European descent; Birch (2012) gives 4 to 6.5% of men of Chinese and Japanese ethnicity. The red-green types are nearly all colour-blind players. Deuteranomaly alone is five of the eight percent: about one man in twelve is colour-blind, and one in twenty has the mildest common form.

For design, the useful fact is this: a colour pair that a dichromat can tell apart, the anomalous trichromat of the same type can too. In every pair we tested that the dichromat model rates below 15, the anomalous model rates it as far apart or farther (within 1). So a palette built for deuteranopia also serves deuteranomaly, and a severity slider has nothing to adjust.

2 · Method

How this was tested

Simulation. Dichromats: Brettel, Viénot and Mollon (1997), with DaltonLens's constants for sRGB. Anomalous trichromats: Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) at severity 0.6. Monochrome: luminance only. All three run in linear RGB; DaltonLens's review shows that skipping that step gives wrong results.

Distance. CIEDE2000 (ΔE), the standard measure of how different two colours look; the implementation reproduces three of Sharma, Wu and Dalal's published test pairs.

The bar. The Okabe–Ito palette, the usual colour-blind-safe set (Wong 2011), keeps its closest pair at 11.7 for deuteranopes. So below, ● clear means 12 or more (as safe as Okabe–Ito), ◐ weak means 8 to 12, and ○ collides means under 8. That rule is mine, anchored to Okabe–Ito; it is not a published standard. Each row shows the worst pair among the colours in the card.

Limits. Simulations model an average observer. The Xbox guidelines say plainly that they don't replace testing with colour-blind players. And no choice of hue helps monochrome vision: only lightness and shape do.

5 · Elsewhere

What guidelines and other games do

The guidelines agree. The Game Accessibility Guidelines make "no essential information is conveyed by a fixed colour alone" a basic item: use colour as a back-up to text, symbol, pattern or shape, by default or through a setting; offer presets for the common types and custom choices; and avoid recolouring the whole game, which they describe as heavy-handed and poorly received by colour-blind players. Xbox Accessibility Guideline 103 says the same, adds that where colour is the primary channel players should be able to pick the colours, from presets or ideally any colour they like, and shows Forza Horizon 4's Deuteranopia, Protanopia, Tritanopia and High Contrast presets and Black Ops Cold War's per-marker colour pickers.

Filters are out of favour. The accessibility specialist Ian Hamilton, on Tekken 8's filters (December 2023), said they generally don't help, and that "filters aren't a fix for colourblindness".

The phone already has one. Android's Colour correction offers Deuteranomaly, Protanomaly, Tritanomaly and Greyscale with an intensity slider; iOS Color Filters offer Protanopia, Deuteranopia, Tritanopia and Greyscale with intensity; Windows 11 has red-green and blue-yellow filters and greyscale. A Rolehack filter would duplicate all three.

Roguelikes. Caves of Qud has options, labelled for colour blindness, to turn off full-screen colour effects and to show tinkering bits as letters instead of coloured dots; the second is on by default. Brogue CE has a "hide color effects" mode. Dwarf Fortress players swap colors.txt; its wiki keeps a "Chromatically Challenged" scheme and a red-green variant. Cataclysm: DDA has a colour manager with themes, and colour-blind presets are an open request (issue 57589, 2022). Crawl's options guide has regex menu and message colours but no palette remap.

NetHack 5.0 itself can recolour any map glyph: OPTIONS=glyph:G_pool/U+224B/0-0-160 (the Guidebook's own example) sets a symbol and colour, the colour as R-G-B or a name from coloratt.c's table (Guidebook, "Customizing Map Glyph Representations"). Both our builds parse it (ENHANCED_SYMBOLS is on) but neither draws it: winandroid.c and winshim.c send gm.sym.color and ignore gm.customcolor, which tty and curses honour. MENUCOLOR and hilite_status take only the 16 basic colours, with bold, underline, inverse and blink as extra cues.

6 · The two routes

A safe default, or a setting per type

Route 1: one of our defaults, safe as it ships

Done literally, as one safe skin, this touches the least risky part: keys, which are already labelled. The colours that fail live in the shared layer that every skin draws: HP, badges, menu colours, the map palette.

Done as safe defaults, it fixes that layer for everyone: the badge tiers get a style each, HP and the outline change hue, and the web's palette gets the phone's spread of lightness. No setting to find, and redundancy helps players with normal vision too (a phone in sunlight, a tired eye). The cost: small visible changes for everyone, and some convention (green for full health, green for blessed) may move.

Route 2: a setting per type and degree

As a filter, it repeats the OS and the guidelines advise against it. As palettes, it does what the OS can't: pick the 16 colours the game's names map to, and the interface's own tokens, for each type. That reaches the ASCII map, menus and messages on both builds without touching the core.

Degrees fall away: a palette safe for the dichromat is safe for the milder form (section 1). Standard names still help, because players look for them. Protanopia and Deuteranopia can share one palette, as Okabe–Ito does, or be tuned apart. Monochrome is mostly second cues plus a lightness-ordered palette.

Layer 1
defaults
Second cues for the five signals and a red-green-safe choice of hues, in every skin, with no setting. Mostly token changes plus the badge styles and the outline stroke. Small.
Layer 2
setting
Colour vision: Standard, Protanopia, Deuteranopia, Tritanopia, Monochrome. Each swaps the 16-colour game palette (winandroid.c's palette[], web.js's COLORS) and the interface tokens. In Settings on the phone, in the menu on the web. Medium.
Layer 3
vanilla
Draw gm.customcolor in both ports, so a player's own glyph: lines work, and ship a few worked examples as comments in defaults.nh. Port-only, as tty does it. Small.
Tiles
Leave them for now. Far look and autodescribe name any monster, and molds don't move. A per-type tile palette (the tile files share one table of 29 colours) or marks on the %%NLOST%% recoloured pairs can follow if a colour-blind playtester asks. Medium to large, and an art call.

8 · For Lucas

Decisions

  1. Safe defaults for red-green, in every skin? Second cues for the five signals and red-green-safe hues, with no setting to turn on.My lean: yes. It covers nearly all colour-blind players and costs the others very little.
  2. HP colours: A (blue, yellow, vermillion), B (plain, yellow, vermillion), or keep green, amber and red and add only the inverse step and the outline's double stroke?My lean: B. Colour appears only when something is wrong, which also suits the terminal look.
  3. Menu colours: change the names in defaults.nh for everyone (blessed becomes cyan, both builds), or keep the names and change only the web's RGB to the phone's lightness spread?My lean: fix the web's RGB now; move blessed to cyan only inside the Colour vision setting, since green-for-blessed is a habit many players share.
  4. The Colour vision setting: which entries (Standard, Protanopia, Deuteranopia, Tritanopia, Monochrome), and one red-green palette behind two names or two tuned palettes? No intensity slider: the phone's own Colour correction has one.My lean: all five names, one shared red-green palette to start, split only if a tester asks.
  5. Vanilla's glyph: option: make both ports draw its custom colours (and later its symbols)?My lean: yes. It is port-only, matches tty, and lets a player fix any pair we missed.
  6. Tiles: leave them, a per-type tile palette, or marks on the recoloured families?My lean: leave them for now, and revisit with a tester.
  7. A colour-blind playtester: about one man in twelve. Is there one among our playtesters or their friends?Simulation got us this far; a real player should sign off before we call it done.
