Colour Vision Tiles

Monsters drawn as one sprite in different colours can merge for a colour-blind player on tiles: in simulation, 11 such pairs merge for red-green vision, 8 for tritanopia and 51 in monochrome. Layer 2 swapped the text map's 16 colours per Colour vision mode; the tiles draw from their own table of 29 colours, so each mode could swap those too. Below: what that does to the families, and to everything else.

The regular palette is not touched. Standard, the default, keeps today's tiles exactly. The palettes below apply only while a player has chosen a Colour vision mode in Settings, as layer 2's text colours do.

Decided (Lucas, 2026-09-28): tile palettes in every Colour vision mode, never in Standard; red-green at the strong setting, the only one that keeps every family apart; tritan at the gentle one, which already does; monochrome at the strong one; the 12 pairs it leaves need marks drawn on their tiles, which are not drawn yet. Built into both apps the same day, and marked beta on 2026-10-08 until a colour-blind player has checked it.

Correction, 2026-10-08: the counts below call a pair merged when the simulated view keeps under 40% of the difference a typical eye sees with the same palette. The palettes partly met that by narrowing what typical eyes see, so the counts flatter them. Measured colour by colour, most differences do widen, some a long way (for protanopes, orange and green dragons go from almost identical to clearly apart), but a few pairs, mostly dragons, stay close, and a few get a little closer (in tritanopia, white and yellow baby dragons).

1 · The idea

A tile palette per mode

The tile files (win/share/monsters.txt, objects.txt, other.txt) share one table of 29 colours. For each mode a search moved the 11 coloured entries so that monsters sharing a letter stay apart in that mode's vision, measured over the whole tile. The background, black, the greys, the whites and skin stay as they are; the paper doll retints skin, so it must not move. It ran over all 161 same-letter pairs whose tiles share a silhouette, at up to three strengths: how far (in CIEDE2000) each colour may move from its own look.

A pair "merges" when the mode's viewer loses more than 60% of the difference a normal eye sees. "Worst pair keeps" is the share the worst pair keeps.

5 · Decisions

What Lucas was asked, and what he chose

  1. Tile palettes in the Colour vision modes? Both builds would swap the tile sheet's pixels when the mode changes: the phone's tile PNG is 98.7% these 29 colours, and the rest can follow the nearest one. The paper doll can follow too: it is built from tile pixels, and its own sprites are written in the same palette letters (RhDoll.java's palette table, the web's doll.js), so they take the same swap. Standard, the default, keeps today's tiles exactly; the swap happens only in the four Colour vision modes.My lean: yes. It completes what layer 2 started, and a colour-blind player on tiles gets the same help as one on the text map.Decided: yes, only inside the modes. Standard keeps the regular palette everyone knows.
  2. Which strength? Compare row 2 across the sheets: that is how the art looks to anyone else in the mode. A third route: the gentle palette, which barely changes the art, plus marks drawn on the few tiles it leaves merged (orange and green dragons, red mold).My lean: %%LEAN%% If keeping the art matters more, the gentle palette plus those marks.Decided: strong for red-green. By the same rule (the smallest shift that keeps every family apart) tritan takes the gentle palette, and monochrome the strong one, which leaves the fewest pairs.
  3. Monochrome: the palette helps with some pairs but not all. The rest need marks drawn on those tiles: a pattern or a small shape per family.My lean: ship the palette now, and draw marks on the remaining pairs later.Decided: as leaned. The palette is in; the marks are not drawn yet.
  4. Tritanopia: its palette changes little, since those families mostly separate already.My lean: include it, so every mode behaves the same way.Decided: included.
