Web port: the layout rule can keep the header stacked in the glass

The twin banks go onto the page next (Lucas, 2026-10-02: "Layout: twin
banks / classic", twin by default).  The page keeps the message and
status bands stacked in #glass, messages over the status, as classic has
them, until it can lay the bands out apart.  The rule's default ('auto')
puts the header side by side once the glass is 818 dp wide, and over the
banks when that shows more of the level, which #glass cannot draw today.
So the page asks for header 'stacked', and the rule then never lays the
bands side by side or over the banks.  On every phone at the default cell
the header is stacked in the glass anyway, and those windows lay out as
before.

- layout.js: DEFAULTS.header, 'auto' or 'stacked'; overFit() and
  header() keep the bands stacked in the glass when it is 'stacked', and
  settled() takes anything else as 'auto'.  The design folder's copy
  (v2) has the same edit, so the two still differ only in their header
  comment.
- test/layout.test.mjs: a test that, with header 'stacked', every usable
  result has two bands, stacked and inside the glass, over phones,
  tablets, foldables and desks, touch and mouse, three settings each;
  and that a window whose header is stacked by default anyway lays out
  exactly as it does by default.
- test/README.md: says so.

Checked: "node --test win/web/test/" passes 134/134 on node 22.22.  The
design's CI gate, "RH_LAYOUT=win/web/layout.js node checks/sweep.mjs" in
the design folder, runs 73,470 layouts in 10 sections with 0 issues.  The
golden fixtures are unchanged: the default is still 'auto'.
